As players develop their character’s background, they should consider what their life was like before joining the [[Mycorris Expedition]]. Did they come from difficult circumstances and struggle to survive? Did they live a comfortable life with access to education and resources? Did they come from a position of influence or privilege?

Even a simple idea, such as a character who was once the heir to a wealthy family, a former military officer, or a researcher seeking recognition, can provide many ideas for developing a character’s history. A character’s background can help explain their skills, their reasons for joining the expedition, and how their [[Obligation]] and [[Motivation]] were formed.

Backgrounds are not mechanical aspects of character development. Instead, they are thematic elements that help define a character beyond their characteristics, skills, and talents. A background provides context for who a character was before Mycorris and what they hope to accomplish on the frontier.

The following backgrounds provide examples of where characters came from, what shaped them, and what brought them to the Mycorris Expedition.

## SOCIOECONOMIC BEGINNINGS

A character's socioeconomic beginnings describe the circumstances they were born into or the position they occupied before becoming an adventurer. Wealth, opportunity, social status, and access to education can shape a character just as strongly as any specific profession or life event.

These backgrounds are intentionally broad. A character's socioeconomic beginnings do not determine what they became, but they can help explain the opportunities available to them, the hardships they experienced, and the expectations they carried into their adult life.

### THE DOWN AND OUT

The character comes from humble or hardscrabble beginnings, growing up with limited resources and few opportunities. They learned early that survival often depends on determination, resourcefulness, and the ability to make do with whatever is available.

Characters from this background strive to better themselves and escape the circumstances that defined their early lives. Some train relentlessly, teaching themselves whatever skills they can, while others rely on toughness, practical experience, and a willingness to take risks. The expedition may represent their greatest opportunity to build a better life—or simply a chance to leave poverty behind.

### MIDDLE CLASS STRUGGLES

The character comes from modest but relatively comfortable circumstances. Their life may have been stable, but it was built on hard work, long hours, and the constant need to keep earning enough to maintain that stability.

This background can include skilled technicians, minor officials, professional soldiers, merchants, tradespeople, educators, or countless other occupations that provide a respectable living without great wealth or influence. The character may have joined the expedition because they wanted something more, found themselves unable to maintain their old life, or saw the frontier as an opportunity to turn their skills into something greater.

### THE HIGH AND MIGHTY

The character comes from wealth, influence, or social status well above that of the average citizen. They may have been born into a powerful family or built a successful life as a wealthy business owner, doctor, politician, executive, or other prominent professional.

A character from this background may have enjoyed comfort and privilege before the expedition, but wealth does not guarantee security. They may have lost their fortune, fallen from favor, become entangled in scandal, or simply decided that life among the powerful was no longer enough. The frontier offers a chance to prove themselves without relying on their family name, connections, or accumulated wealth.

### THE OUTSIDER

The character comes from a society, culture, or community far removed from the galactic mainstream. They may have grown up on an isolated world, within a secluded culture, or among people whose traditions and technology differ significantly from those found throughout the galaxy.

As a result, the character may be unfamiliar with the politics, customs, technology, and social expectations of more established worlds. They may approach unfamiliar things with suspicion or superstition, or embrace the wider galaxy with curiosity and wonder. The expedition can be their first real opportunity to experience a galaxy much larger than the one they knew.

## CORE BACKGROUNDS

Core Backgrounds describe major circumstances, experiences, or turning points that shaped a character before the campaign begins. Unlike Career-specific backgrounds, they are available to characters regardless of their profession or specialization.

A Core Background can be combined with a character's socioeconomic beginnings and Career to create a more complete history. The character might come from a wealthy family but have been driven into the expedition by enemies, or grow up in poverty before seeing an opportunity to build a better life. These backgrounds are starting points rather than complete histories, giving players room to develop the details that make their character unique.

### OPPORTUNITY KNOCKS

The character sees opportunity where others see uncertainty. They may be seeking wealth, adventure, knowledge, a new career, or simply a chance to test themselves beyond the life they already know.

Perhaps they heard about the expedition and recognized an opportunity too good to ignore, or perhaps they have always wanted to see what lies beyond the established worlds. Whatever the reason, the character believes that remaining where they are would mean missing the chance of a lifetime.

### A HIGHER CALLING

Something convinced the character that they were meant to leave their former life behind. This calling might come from a personal belief, a vision, a religious conviction, a sense of duty, or simply an overwhelming feeling that they have a greater purpose elsewhere.

The character may not fully understand what awaits them on Mycorris, but they believe the expedition is part of a larger purpose. Whether that belief proves correct or not, the calling gives them a reason to endure the dangers and uncertainties of the journey.

### ENEMIES AND ANTAGONISTS

The character's former life became impossible because they made an enemy. Perhaps they crossed the wrong person, challenged a powerful organization, exposed a secret, or simply found themselves on the wrong side of a personal feud.

They may be running from someone who wants revenge, seeking protection from a powerful adversary, or hoping that distance will finally allow them to live without looking over their shoulder. The expedition offers a place where they can disappear—but their enemies may not be as easy to leave behind as they hoped.

### A FAILURE OF CHARACTER

The character's own actions caused them to abandon the life they once knew. Perhaps they made a serious mistake, betrayed someone's trust, gave in to a destructive habit, or allowed ambition, pride, greed, or fear to ruin an important part of their life.

The character may regret what happened, deny responsibility, or believe they were justified. Regardless, the consequences have followed them. Joining the expedition gives them an opportunity to start over, prove themselves, or escape the person they became.

### WRONG PLACE, WRONG TIME

The character never intended to become an adventurer. Circumstances simply pushed them into a life they never expected.

They may have witnessed something they were never meant to see, become caught in a political dispute, survived a disaster, been mistaken for someone else, or found themselves involved in events far beyond their control. The expedition may have been the safest—or only—way forward. Now that they have come this far, they must decide what to do with the unexpected life that circumstances have given them.
