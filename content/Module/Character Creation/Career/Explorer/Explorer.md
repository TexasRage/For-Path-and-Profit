The Explorer seeks out the unknown. They chart new routes, investigate unexplored worlds, study alien cultures, and gather information that others are willing to pay for. Some work for governments or corporations, while others operate independently, selling their knowledge and expertise to whoever needs it. The Explorer’s eight Career Skills are **Astrogation, Cool, Knowledge (Lore), Knowledge (Outer Rim), Knowledge (Xenology), Perception, Piloting (Space), and Survival**. The character automatically gains **one rank in four** of these skills of their choice without spending starting experience.

An Explorer is a natural fit for the expedition, possessing the skills needed to navigate unfamiliar space and survive beyond established civilization. They may join to discover something no one has seen before, chart new hyperspace routes, study the unknown, or build their reputation by gathering valuable information. Whatever their reason, the expedition offers exactly what an Explorer wants: somewhere new to go.

## EXPLORER BACKGROUNDS

An Explorer rarely sets out into the unknown without a reason. The decision to join an expedition may come from curiosity, ambition, desperation, or a desire to escape the life one already knows. A character's background helps establish what brought him to that decision and what he hopes to find beyond the familiar worlds of the galaxy.

These backgrounds are intended as starting points for developing an Explorer's history. They should help explain why the character was willing to leave behind whatever life he had before and accept the risks that come with traveling into unknown space.

### ACADEMIC

For an academic, an expedition offers something no library, university, or laboratory can provide: firsthand knowledge. The galaxy contains countless worlds, civilizations, species, and phenomena that remain poorly understood, and some questions simply cannot be answered from a distance. An academic may join to conduct research, gather data, study unfamiliar cultures, or investigate phenomena that have never been properly documented.

For some, the expedition is the culmination of years of study; for others, it is the beginning of an entirely new field of research. They may also seek freedom from institutions that restrict their work or theories. Whatever the reason, the Academic believes the knowledge waiting beyond the frontier is worth the dangers required to obtain it.

### CRIMINAL

For a criminal, an expedition can provide distance from the law, old enemies, and the consequences of past mistakes. The farther an expedition travels from established civilization, the fewer questions are likely to be asked about someone’s history. A criminal might join because authorities are searching for them, a former employer wants them dead, or they simply need to disappear before someone catches up.

Others see the frontier as an opportunity. Remote worlds may offer access to illegal goods, stolen technology, valuable resources, or opportunities unavailable in civilized space. Some criminals may simply view the expedition as another way to profit, taking advantage of the frontier’s dangers and lack of established law.

### ENTREPRENEUR

Entrepreneurs see opportunity where others see uncertainty. An unexplored world may contain valuable resources, new markets, profitable trade routes, or discoveries that have yet to reach the wider galaxy. An Entrepreneur might join an expedition because they believe a fortune is waiting at its destination, hoping to secure valuable resources, establish a new trade route, or claim an opportunity before larger competitors arrive.

Others simply see the frontier as a place to build something from nothing. With fewer established businesses and competitors, an ambitious Entrepreneur can establish a new enterprise, develop a valuable resource, or become an important figure in a growing settlement. To an Entrepreneur, an expedition is not merely a journey into the unknown—it is an investment.

### FORMER AUTHORITY AGENT

Not everyone who once served a powerful organization remains within it. Some leave willingly, others are forced out, and some discover that their continued service conflicts with their conscience, safety, or future. A former authority agent might join an expedition to escape their past and build a new identity far from the records, expectations, and institutions that once defined them.

Others leave with unfinished business. They may have uncovered something they were never meant to see, possess knowledge others want buried, or seek answers that cannot safely be pursued within established space. For some, the expedition simply represents freedom—the chance to leave behind years of hierarchy, procedure, and obligation and travel beyond the reach of former superiors.

### FALLEN NOBLE

For a fallen noble, the frontier offers an opportunity rarely found in the Core Worlds: the chance to be judged by what they can accomplish rather than by family reputation. A Noble may join an expedition after losing their family’s favor, fortune, or political standing, whether through scandal, misfortune, or their own choices. Others simply want to prove they can succeed without relying on their family’s name.

An expedition may also provide a path toward rebuilding a lost fortune. Valuable discoveries, new territory, and untapped resources offer opportunities for someone willing to take risks that established society would never consider. For others, it is a matter of pride: having lost everything that once defined them, they want to accomplish something that cannot be dismissed as an inheritance.

### WANDERER

Some people simply cannot remain in one place. Curiosity, boredom, tragedy, restlessness, or a desire for something more can drive a person away from home and onto the hyperlanes. A Wanderer may join an expedition because it offers another destination and another reason to keep moving. The promise of an unexplored world may be far more appealing than settling somewhere familiar.

Others have spent years traveling from world to world, taking whatever work they could find and following opportunities wherever they lead. An expedition can provide structure to a life defined by constant movement. For some, however, the journey represents something more: a search for a place to call home, something lost long ago, or a sense of purpose they have never found elsewhere.

## EXPLORER OBLIGATIONS

Explorers often have complicated relationships with Obligation. Their work may depend on sponsors, employers, governments, academic institutions, or other patrons who provide the resources needed to reach distant worlds. Others are tied to their families, companions, creditors, or the crews who depend on them. These relationships can follow an Explorer long after they leave familiar space. For some Explorers, the expedition itself is an obligation. A character may be responsible for reporting discoveries, repaying a sponsor, protecting their companions, or completing a mission others have heavily invested in. Others may have joined to escape old debts, rivals, or responsibilities, only to find that distance does not make them disappear.

Explorers may also develop new obligations as they travel. A discovery may attract unwanted attention, a sponsor may demand exclusive rights to valuable information, or a stranded expedition may leave the characters responsible for people who depend on them. The farther an Explorer travels, the more connections they make—and the harder those connections can be to leave behind. Obligation defines what an Explorer has left behind, who depends on them, and what responsibilities continue to shape their choices. Players should consider how their Obligation influenced their decision to join the expedition and what they may face upon reaching Mycorris. Players may use [[Explorer Obligations]] in place of [[Core Obligations]], either rolling randomly or selecting one that fits their character. Characters begin with the normal amount of Obligation based on group size and may take on additional Obligation to gain extra starting experience or credits.

## EXPLORER MOTIVATIONS

Explorers are driven by the desire to see what lies beyond the known galaxy. Their Motivation may come from curiosity, scientific interest, a thirst for adventure, the search for knowledge, or the desire to make a name for themselves. An Explorer's Motivation helps explain why they are willing to travel into unknown regions, face unfamiliar dangers, and pursue discoveries that others might consider too risky or insignificant.

Explorers may roll on the [[Types of Motivation]] table or choose a Motivation that fits their character. The career-specific result is **Discovery**. If a character has Discovery as their Motivation, they may then choose or roll for a specific Discovery on the [[Specific Discoveries]] table. These options represent the different goals that can drive an Explorer, from charting unknown worlds and uncovering lost civilizations to advancing scientific knowledge, finding valuable resources, or becoming the first to discover something no one has seen before.
