| d100   | Specific Obligation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 01-08  | **Antagonist:** The Smuggler has insulted, betrayed, or otherwise angered someone who now wants to make their life difficult. This may be a former employer, rival, criminal organization, or simply someone with enough influence to cause trouble.                                              |
| 09-16  | **Bad Reputation:** The Smuggler has developed a reputation for being unreliable, dishonest, or difficult to deal with. Finding good work may be difficult until they prove themselves again, forcing them to accept less desirable jobs or repair their reputation through successful contracts. |
| 17-24  | **Betrayal:** The Smuggler has been betrayed by someone they once trusted, or they betrayed someone else. They may seek revenge, make amends, or attempt to repair a relationship that their actions helped destroy.                                                                              |
| 25-32  | **Bounty:** There is a price on the Smuggler's head. The bounty might have been placed by a criminal organization, former employer, government, or private individual, and the danger it presents depends on who is offering the reward.                                                          |
| 33-40  | **Criminal:** The Smuggler has a criminal record or is wanted for a crime. They may be unable to safely enter certain systems, forced to conceal their identity, or determined to prove that they were wrongly accused.                                                                           |
| 41-48  | **Debt:** The Smuggler owes a significant debt, perhaps for their ship, equipment, cargo, or a favor that kept their business alive. The debt continues to grow more troublesome the longer it remains unpaid, particularly if the creditor is unwilling to wait.                                 |
| 49-56  | **Family:** The Smuggler has family members who depend on them or have become entangled in their criminal life. They may provide supplies and credits to relatives, protect them from enemies, or be forced to cooperate with criminals who threaten their loved ones.                            |
| 57-64  | **Favor:** The Smuggler owes a favor to someone who helped them in the past. Favors are common currency among Smugglers, and the character may owe several people whose interests do not always align. Eventually, someone will come calling to collect.                                          |
| 65-72  | **Notoriety:** The Smuggler is too well known in certain circles to travel unnoticed. Their name or appearance may be recognized by authorities, criminals, former clients, or rivals, making jobs more difficult and potentially attracting unwanted attention.                                  |
| 73-80  | **High Risk:** The Smuggler cannot resist dangerous jobs or high-stakes opportunities. They have something to prove and may accept risky work simply because someone doubts they can pull it off, potentially putting themselves and their companions in danger.                                  |
| 81-88  | **Servitude:** The Smuggler is bound to another person or organization. They may owe their freedom, ship, or livelihood to a powerful master and must surrender earnings or complete assignments until they can escape, buy their freedom, or otherwise end the arrangement.                      |
| 89-96  | **Under Contract:** The Smuggler is bound by an agreement to transport cargo, obtain rare goods, provide a service, or complete another difficult task. The contract may be legitimate or criminal, but ignoring it can bring serious consequences.                                               |
| 97-100 | Roll twice on this chart. Starting Obligation is split into two different origins. (This does not increase the Obligation's magnitude; divide the starting Obligation into two equal parts, each with a different type).                                                                          |
