Obligation represents the responsibilities, problems, and personal ties that a character carries with them into the Mycorris Expedition. These obligations can be tangible, such as a debt owed, a binding contract, or a promise that must be fulfilled. They can also be personal, such as responsibility for another person, loyalty to an organization, or a duty that the character cannot ignore.

A character’s Obligation helps define who they are beyond their skills and abilities. It can influence their decisions, shape their relationships, and provide the Game Master with opportunities to connect the character’s personal story to the challenges of the expedition.

During character creation, each player selects a starting Obligation for their character. An Obligation may come from their Background, their reason for joining the expedition, their connection to the expedition’s resources, or events from their previous life.

Examples of Obligation include:

- A debt owed to someone who helped secure a place on the expedition.
- A responsibility to protect a family member, companion, or community.
- A contract or agreement with an organization sponsoring the expedition.
- A promise to uncover the secrets of Mycorris.
- A past mistake, enemy, or unresolved conflict following the character.

Throughout the campaign, Obligations can create challenges and story opportunities. Characters may work to resolve their Obligations, maintain them, or accept additional responsibilities in exchange for new opportunities.

## WHAT IS OBLIGATION?

Each character begins play with at least one Obligation. An Obligation consists of two parts:

- Narrative Description: The story behind the Obligation. This explains what the character owes, who is involved, and why it matters. The description has no direct mechanical effect but helps connect the character to the campaign.
- Obligation Value: The numerical size of the Obligation. This determines its mechanical impact during play.

When selecting an Obligation, players may roll randomly on [[Core Obligations]] table or the appropriate Career-Specific Obligation table. Alternatively, they may choose an option that better fits their character’s Background and story, with Game Master approval.

During character creation, players may have either one or two Obligations, dividing their total starting Obligation value between them. If a character begins with two Obligations, each may be selected from either the Core Obligation table or their Career-Specific Obligation table. The two Obligations may come from the same table or from different tables.

### STARTING OBLIGATION

Each character begins play with a starting Obligation determined by the number of players in the group, as shown on the Starting Obligation table. This Obligation represents a significant responsibility, problem, or personal connection that follows the character into the Mycorris Expedition.

Players may choose to increase their character’s starting Obligation in exchange for additional experience points or credits for starting equipment. However, taking on additional Obligation increases the pressure placed on both the character and the expedition when Obligations are triggered during play. There are two limitations to this: each player can only choose each option once, and Player Characters cannot gain more additional Obligation than their original starting value.

## OBLIGATION IN PLAY

In addition to its narrative role, Obligation also has mechanical effects. Before each session, the Game Master rolls percentile dice and compares the result to the group's total current Obligation.

### OBLIGATION CHECK RESULTS

If the result is higher than the group's total Obligation, none of the characters' Obligations significantly affect the session.

If the result is equal to or less than the group's total Obligation, one or more Obligations begin to weigh on the group. All Player Characters reduce their strain threshold by 1 for the remainder of the session.

The Game Master then determines whose Obligation was triggered by comparing the roll to the group's Obligation chart. The character whose Obligation is triggered instead reduces their strain threshold by 2 for the remainder of the session.

If the triggering roll is doubles (such as 11, 44, or 88), the effects are intensified. All Player Characters reduce their strain threshold by 2 for the session, while the character whose Obligation was triggered reduces their strain threshold by 4.

These mechanical effects represent the stress and pressure created by a character's unresolved Obligations. A debt may come due, a favor may be called in, a contract may demand immediate attention, an addiction may create powerful cravings, or a family responsibility may suddenly require action. The Game Master should use the triggered Obligation to introduce complications that naturally connect to the character's story and the events of the adventure.

Ultimately, even though these mechanical effects always come into play, it is up to the Game Master to determine how they are expressed narratively. The GM should not feel obligated to disrupt their planned story or ongoing adventure in order to represent a triggered Obligation in a literal way. The effects of a triggered Obligation can always be internal or psychological in nature.

For example, if a character’s “bounty” Obligation is triggered during an ongoing adventure, but introducing bounty hunters would derail the current narrative, the Game Master may instead represent the strain as mental pressure. The character might suffer a reduced strain threshold because they are distracted, anxious, or worried that their current actions are increasing the risk of being discovered, rather than an external confrontation occurring immediately.

### HOMESTEAD OBLIGATION

Once the Mycorris Expedition establishes a permanent homestead, the settlement becomes an additional responsibility for the group. The Homestead adds 5 points of Obligation to the group's total Obligation, representing the ongoing effort required to maintain the settlement, protect its inhabitants, and secure its resources.

As the homestead grows, additional colonists and expanded operations create new responsibilities. Each increase in the settlement's population may increase the Homestead Obligation as the expedition becomes responsible for more lives, supplies, and infrastructure. See [[Chapter X: Homestead Management]] for more information on how population growth affects Homestead Obligation.

The Homestead Obligation represents the challenges of maintaining a settlement on an unexplored world. Unlike personal Obligations, which represent the debts, promises, and responsibilities carried by individual characters, Homestead Obligation represents the needs and vulnerabilities of the expedition as a whole.

When the Homestead Obligation is triggered, roll on [[Table 4-X: Homestead Trigger Events]] instead of determining an individual character's Obligation. These events represent problems that threaten the stability of the settlement and require the expedition's attention.

Homestead Trigger Events may include issues with essential resources, environmental hazards, security threats, or unexpected complications. The Game Master should use these events as opportunities to encourage exploration, resource management, and cooperation among the expedition members.

## OBLIGATION AS A THRESHOLD

Obligation can also represent how others perceive a character or the Mycorris Expedition as a whole. Depending on the nature of their Obligations, a high or low Obligation value may affect how groups, organizations, and individuals respond to them.

A high Obligation does not always represent a negative reputation. It may indicate that the characters are heavily committed, widely known, or connected to powerful groups. However, it can also mean they are burdened by unresolved debts, obligations, or past mistakes.

The Game Master may establish Obligation thresholds that influence access to certain opportunities. Some organizations may hesitate to work with an expedition carrying too many unresolved responsibilities, while others may value characters with strong connections, experience, or influence.

For example, a research organization may require an expedition to have a low Obligation before granting access to restricted information, while a corporation may be more willing to negotiate with a group that has existing contracts and obligations.

The higher a group's Obligation becomes, the more important those commitments become in shaping how others interact with the expedition.

## OBLIGATION AS A RESOURCE

Characters may voluntarily accept additional Obligation to gain access to resources, opportunities, or assistance that would otherwise be unavailable.

Additional Obligation may represent accepting a contract, making a promise, requesting support from an organization, or taking on responsibility for additional personnel or equipment. The Game Master determines the amount of Obligation gained based on the significance of the benefit and the consequences attached to it.

Obligation does not have a fixed value in credits. The value depends on the circumstances surrounding the agreement. A small favor from a close ally may create little Obligation, while accepting major support from a powerful organization may create a much greater responsibility.

Examples include:

- Accepting funding from a corporation in exchange for future services.
- Taking responsibility for additional colonists joining the homestead.
- Receiving specialized equipment in exchange for completing a future task.
- Requesting emergency support from a faction that expects repayment.

### SETTLING OBLIGATION

When characters fulfill their responsibilities or resolve the problems connected to their Obligations, they may reduce their current Obligation. This is known as settling Obligation.

Settling an Obligation may occur through specific actions tied directly to its source or through general progress that provides the resources needed to resolve multiple concerns.

#### SPECIFIC SETTLEMENT

A specific settlement resolves an individual Obligation through an action directly connected to its source.

For example, a character with a Contract Obligation may reduce it by completing the agreed work, while a character with a Debt Obligation may reduce it by making payments or providing services.

#### GENERIC SETTLEMENT

In some cases, the expedition gains resources or opportunities that can be applied toward multiple Obligations. This may include earning credits, gaining political support, completing major objectives, or creating enough stability to address ongoing problems.

The Game Master determines how much Obligation is reduced, and the players decide how those reductions are applied among their characters and the Homestead.
