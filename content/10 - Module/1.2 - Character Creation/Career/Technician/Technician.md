Technicians are knowledgeable and practical, specializing in the operation, maintenance, repair, and modification of technology. Their eight career skills are **Astrogation, Computers, Coordination, Discipline, Knowledge (Outer Rim), Mechanics, Perception, and Piloting (Planetary)**. They automatically gain **one rank in four** of these skills of their choosing without spending experience, and receive a discount when spending experience to purchase ranks in any of these skills. A Technician can be found almost anywhere machines are used, from remote settlements and farms to starships and major facilities, keeping essential equipment running and finding ways to improve it.

Technicians often take greater pride in their work than their appearance, preferring machines and droids to the unpredictability of people. Their expertise makes them valuable throughout the Outer Rim, where reliable technical support can be difficult to find. Criminal organizations, businesses, expeditions, and frontier settlements all have use for someone who can repair damaged equipment, maintain complex machinery, or create useful modifications. A Technician's reputation is often built on the quality of their work, and a particularly skilled Technician may find steady employment simply by proving that they can keep an operation's most important equipment running.

## TECHNICIAN BACKGROUNDS

Technicians can come from virtually any background. Some are self-taught, developing their skills through personal interest or necessity, while others receive formal training through apprenticeships, vocational programs, businesses, or military service. They may be respected craftspeople, dedicated engineers, vehicle enthusiasts, data specialists, or criminals who use their technical knowledge to support illicit operations.

Every Technician came from somewhere, and the experiences of their earlier life helped shape the person they became. A Background provides story details that can help define how a character thinks, works, and interacts with others, while giving the Game Master opportunities to connect the character's past to the campaign. Players should combine their chosen Background with their skills, talents, Motivation, and Obligation to establish the foundation of their character, while allowing that identity to develop as the campaign unfolds.

### ANTIQUARIAN

While most Technicians focus on advancing technology, Antiquarians look to the past for inspiration. They believe older methods and forgotten designs still hold value, and that rediscovering them can lead to innovations just as meaningful as creating something entirely new. An Antiquarian might restore ancient starship engines, adapt obsolete components for modern systems, or scour derelict worlds for lost schematics and forgotten techniques.

To an Antiquarian, progress is not simply about invention—it is about understanding the lineage of technology. They study outdated programming languages, obsolete machinery, forgotten manufacturing techniques, and archaic designs, preserving knowledge that might otherwise disappear. By combining old craftsmanship with modern insight, Antiquarians keep the wisdom of the past alive while finding new uses for technology that others have discarded.

### BORN ENGINEER

Born Engineers are Technicians whose mechanical aptitude manifested early in life. They may have grown up surrounded by machinery, learned from a mentor, or simply discovered a natural talent for understanding how things work. Their curiosity drives them to take devices apart, study their construction, and rebuild them better than before.

Regardless of how their talent developed, Born Engineers share a deep connection to technology. They may have learned their craft in a family workshop, aboard a starship, or through years of experimenting with machines, droids, or computer systems. Their natural ability can lead them toward perfection, innovation, or simply the satisfaction of creating something with their own hands.

### TECH DESIGNER

Tech Designers are visionaries who see technology as a means of transformation. They work at the cutting edge, creating devices and systems that redefine what is possible. To them, technology can be both a practical tool and a form of creative expression, and they are willing to challenge established methods in pursuit of new ideas.

Their work may involve developing cybernetic enhancements, modifying starships and weapons, experimenting with unconventional technology, or creating new ways to manipulate and process information. Some push the boundaries of what machines can do, while others explore the relationship between biology and technology. Whatever their field, Tech Designers are driven by the belief that progress comes from experimentation, and that the limitations of today's technology are simply problems waiting to be solved.

### UNDERWORLD TECH EXPERT

Underworld Tech Experts make their living supporting criminal enterprises, fringe organizations, and other operations where technical skill is often more valuable than a clean reputation. They may maintain equipment, repair vehicles, modify technology, or provide specialized technical support for people who cannot rely on legitimate services. Their work often makes them indispensable to the organizations they serve, even if they are rarely given the same recognition as the smugglers, enforcers, or leaders who depend on them.

Their expertise can take many forms. Cyber Techs may maintain cybernetics and keep injured criminals functioning, while Droid Techs repair and modify security or labor droids. Modders can customize ships and vehicles for smuggling, combat, or concealment, and Slicers may breach computer systems, manipulate records, or develop tools for bypassing security. Whatever their specialty, Underworld Tech Experts are accustomed to working with limited resources, questionable clients, and problems that require unconventional solutions. For them, knowing how to make something work—and knowing when not to ask questions—is often more important than following the rules.

## TECHNICIAN OBLIGATIONS

Technicians often find themselves responsible for more than just their own equipment. A machine they repaired, a project they abandoned, an employer they left behind, or a client who still expects results can all become sources of Obligation. Technical work also creates relationships with people who depend on a Technician's skills, whether legitimate businesses, wealthy patrons, criminal organizations, or individuals who simply cannot afford to replace what the Technician keeps running.

A Technician's Obligation may involve debts, unfinished projects, proprietary technology, former employers, or people who rely on their expertise. These burdens can shape the Technician's career and provide opportunities for the Game Master to bring the character's past into the campaign. Players may use [[Technician Obligations]] instead of the [[Core Obligations]] when creating a Technician character. They may roll randomly or choose an Obligation that fits their character's background. Characters may also take additional Obligation at character creation to gain additional starting experience or credits, following the normal rules for doing so.

## TECHNICIAN SPECIALIZATIONS

The Technician career encompasses a broad range of Specializations for characters who earn their livelihood through technical skill and problem-solving. Some focus on repairing and enhancing machinery, while others work with droids, cybernetics, computer systems, or cutting-edge experimental technology. Whether formally educated or self-taught, Technicians rely on knowledge, creativity, and hands-on experience to overcome challenges. Each Specialization reflects a distinct relationship with technology: Mechanics keep equipment running smoothly, Modders enhance and customize gear, Droid Techs maintain and improve artificial beings, Cyber Techs specialize in augmentations, Slicers manipulate digital systems and information networks, and Outlaw Techs operate outside conventional limits to find unconventional solutions. Together, these paths allow Technicians to either specialize deeply in a chosen field or serve as adaptable problem-solvers in any expedition or operation.

## TECHNICIAN MOTIVATIONS

Technicians are often driven by a desire to understand how things work and to discover better ways of doing them. Their work can be motivated by curiosity, ambition, pride, necessity, or the simple satisfaction of solving a problem that others could not. A Technician's Motivation helps explain what keeps them tinkering long after a job is finished and what they hope to accomplish with their knowledge and skills.

Technicians may roll on the [[Types of Motivation]] table or choose a Motivation that fits their character. The career-specific result is Innovation. If a character has Innovation as their Motivation, they may then choose or roll for a specific Innovation on the [[Specific Innovations]] table. These options represent the different ambitions that can drive a Technician, from creating new technology and improving existing designs to recovering lost knowledge or proving that their ideas can change the galaxy.
