Weapons are built to withstand the rigors of combat, but even the finest equipment eventually wears down. A weapon may suffer Damage whenever it is affected by the **Sunder** quality, its wielder generates \[despair] on a combat check, or the GM determines that an environmental hazard or other circumstance damages it.

Weapon Damage is divided into four levels: **Minor**, **Moderate**, **Major**, and **Destroyed**. Each time a weapon suffers Damage, it advances one step along this track and applies the effects of its new Damage level.

#### Damage Levels

| Damage        | Difficulty                                  | Penalty for Use                                                 |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Minor**     | Easy (\[difficulty])                         | The weapon suffers an additional \[setback] on combat checks.    |
| **Moderate**  | Average (\[difficulty]\[difficulty])          | The weapon suffers an additional \[difficulty] on combat checks. |
| **Major**     | Hard (\[difficulty]\[difficulty]\[difficulty]) | Unusable                                                        |
| **Destroyed** | Beyond Repair                               | Beyond Repair                                                   |

A weapon's current Damage level determines the difficulty of using or repairing it. A weapon that reaches **Major** is unusable until repaired.

A weapon that suffers Damage while already at **Major** becomes **Destroyed**. A Destroyed weapon is considered beyond repair and must be replaced.

See [[Weapon Repair]] for rules governing the repair of damaged weapons.
